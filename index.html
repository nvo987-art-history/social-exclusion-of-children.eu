Children · Community · Belonging

Every child deserves to belong.

An international awareness campaign addressing social exclusion within children's peer groups and communities. The initiative seeks to make exclusion more visible and encourage social environments in which every child can participate with dignity.

A place for every voice.
A shared principle

Belonging is not a privilege. Every child should have the opportunity to be included, heard and treated with dignity.

Understanding exclusionWhy it matters

Making the invisible visible.

Social exclusion can begin in ordinary moments: a place left empty, an invitation that never comes, a voice that is repeatedly overlooked.

Within children's peer groups and communities, rejection and isolation can shape a child's sense of belonging, relationships, participation and emotional well-being. Exclusion is not always expressed through open aggression. It can also emerge through subtle, repeated patterns in everyday social life.

The Social Exclusion of Children campaign aims to raise awareness of these dynamics, encourage recognition of exclusionary behaviour and strengthen understanding of children's need for acceptance and meaningful social connection.

Campaign approachFrom awareness to action

Belonging is something we create together.

A more inclusive social world begins with noticing who is missing, listening to what goes unheard and making room for participation.

Recognising exclusion

Making rejection, isolation and less visible forms of exclusion easier to recognise in children's peer groups and everyday communities.

Strengthening inclusion

Encouraging acceptance, mutual respect, belonging and meaningful opportunities to participate in shared social life.

Shared responsibility

Recognising the roles peers, adults and communities can play in creating social environments where every child has a place.

The guiding idea

The guiding idea

No child should have to wonder whether there is room for them. There should be.

The campaign seeks to contribute to a culture in which social exclusion is recognised, challenged and prevented—without reducing the issue to individual incidents alone.
