Every child deserves to belong.

An international awareness campaign addressing social exclusion within children's peer groups and communities. The initiative seeks to make exclusion more visible and to encourage social environments in which every child can participate with dignity.

Project in preparation Planned launch: November 2027
Campaign statement
Recognise exclusion.
Strengthen belonging.

The central principle

Belonging is not a privilege. Every child should have the opportunity to be included, heard and treated with dignity.

01 / The concept

Making everyday exclusion visible.

Social exclusion within children's peer groups and communities can affect their sense of belonging, social relationships, participation and emotional well-being.

Exclusion may take the form of rejection, isolation, being left out of group activities or persistent barriers to meaningful social interaction. It is not always expressed through open aggression; it can also develop through subtle, repeated patterns in everyday social life.

The Social Exclusion of Children campaign aims to raise awareness of these dynamics, encourage recognition of exclusionary behaviour and strengthen understanding of children's need for acceptance and meaningful social connection.

02 / Campaign approach

A clear, focused framework.

The campaign concentrates on three connected areas that support a more informed and inclusive approach to children's social lives.

01

Recognising exclusion

Identifying rejection, isolation and social exclusion within children's peer groups and everyday communities.

02

Strengthening inclusion

Promoting acceptance, mutual respect, belonging and meaningful opportunities to participate in shared social life.

03

Shared responsibility

Recognising the roles peers, adults and communities can play in creating more inclusive social environments.

03 / Guiding principle

Every child deserves to belong, participate and be treated with dignity.

The campaign seeks to contribute to a culture in which social exclusion is recognised, challenged and prevented—without reducing the issue to individual incidents alone.
